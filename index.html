Chartered Professional Accountant · Senior Field Auditor, Ontario Ministry of Finance

Rajan Sharma, CPA

Forty-one years in finance. Thirty-five years in Ontario’s public service. Twenty-five years of community leadership.

Rajan Sharma is a Chartered Professional Accountant and Senior Field Auditor with the Ontario Ministry of Finance, and a member of the Ontario Public Service since 1991. Across 35 years of service — in the Ministries of Health, the Attorney General and Finance — he has specialized in fiscal oversight, statutory compliance and corporate audit, and today manages complex audits of multi-billion-dollar corporations. Between 2024 and 2026 he completed 27 complex corporate audits, identified systemic compliance gaps and recovered $3.2 million in public revenues, resolving multi-million-dollar tax matters through impartial, evidence-based consensus rather than litigation. Throughout his career he has served with strict political neutrality, guided by evidence, statutory compliance and the professional standards of CPA Ontario.

Mr. Sharma immigrated to Canada from New Delhi, India, in 1981 and became a Canadian citizen in 1985. For more than 25 years he has matched his professional service with civic leadership across the Greater Toronto Area and Waterloo Region: mentoring more than 70 newcomers and international students, helping establish a permanent spiritual and cultural centre in Cambridge, Ontario, coordinating cultural programming for South Asians in Ontario and Arya Samaj Markham, and founding the non-profit Bollywood Ki Dhun. His service has been recognized with the Government of Ontario’s 30 Years and 35 Years of Public Service Awards, a commendation letter from the Prime Minister of Canada in 2022 and the Queen Elizabeth II Platinum Jubilee Recognition Pin in 2023. He lives in Markham, Ontario, speaks English, Hindi and Punjabi, and is studying French.

Community

Community initiatives

Seven ways Mr. Sharma has served the communities of Ontario over 25 years, each with the organizations and people involved.

DRSANA-Canada

Mentoring newcomers and international students

Drawing on his own experience as an immigrant, Mr. Sharma has personally mentored and supported more than 70 newcomers and international students through their transition into Canadian life. His pro bono work covers career counselling, housing guidance and educational mentorship.

He has also facilitated transitional family hosting, guided internationally trained professionals toward meaningful employment, and supported benevolent community relief — frontline work that has given him practical insight into housing, workforce integration and community wellness.

Ongoing · Greater Toronto Area and Waterloo Region

DRSANA-Canada · Cambridge, Ontario

A permanent spiritual and cultural centre for Cambridge

In 2017 and 2018, Mr. Sharma facilitated the acquisition of a heritage church in Cambridge, Ontario, establishing a permanent Spiritual and Cultural Centre that now serves a congregation of 300 members.

The project followed a decade of governance service as Secretary of DRSANA’s Toronto Branch (2010–2020), where he oversaw branch governance, weekly assemblies and community outreach.

2017–2018 · Serving 300 members

Bollywood Ki Dhun (The Tune of Bollywood)

Bollywood Ki Dhun: a stage for emerging talent

Mr. Sharma founded the non-profit Bollywood Ki Dhun in 2021 to promote emerging North American musical talent through live and virtual events. Its Kishore Kumar tribute evenings at the Vedic Cultural Centre in Markham, most recently in October 2026 with South Asians in Ontario, bring together music lovers from across the Greater Toronto Area’s South Asian community.

His own musical journey began in Regina, Saskatchewan, between 1982 and 1984. He has since performed across the Greater Toronto Area and on Parliament Hill in Ottawa. In 2022 the Office of the Prime Minister of Canada recognized his leadership in founding the organization and promoting multicultural arts and performing heritage across Ontario.

2021–present · Markham, Ontario

Arts and communityMarkham, Ontario · October 13, 2026

GTA community gathers to honour Kishore Kumar on his 39th death anniversary

Bollywood Ki Dhun and South Asians in Ontario brought music lovers to the Vedic Cultural Centre for an evening of tribute, opened by a traditional lamp-lighting ceremony led by community leaders.

On October 13, 2026, music enthusiasts and members of the Greater Toronto Area’s South Asian community gathered at the Vedic Cultural Centre in Markham to celebrate the legacy of Kishore Kumar on his 39th death anniversary. The event was organized by Bollywood Ki Dhun and South Asians in Ontario.

The evening opened with a traditional diya (lamp-lighting) ceremony conducted by Sam Chopra, President of South Asians in Ontario; Yash Kapur, President of Arya Samaj Markham; Harry Dhingra, President of the Mantra Lions Club; and Rajan Sharma, Founder of Bollywood Ki Dhun.

Mr. Sharma opened the performances with “Chalte Chalte Mere Ye Geet Yaad Rakhna.” The program moved through classics reflecting the singer’s range, including “Zindagi Ka Safar Hai Ye Kaisa Safar” and “Mere Mehboob Qayamat Hogi”; Dr. V. Radhakrishnan performed “Aankhon Aankhon Mein Hum Tum.” The finale, “Yeh Jawani Hai Deewani / Dekha Na Socha Na,” featured live dhol by Gurinder Singh Dhingra alongside Mr. Sharma and brought the audience to its feet.

In a keynote address, guest speaker Raj Arora traced Kishore Kumar’s legacy as a playback singer who introduced yodelling to Hindi cinema, holder of a record eight Filmfare Awards for Best Male Playback Singer, and an actor, director, producer and composer. Mr. Arora also reflected on Mr. Sharma’s own musical journey, which began in Regina, Saskatchewan, between 1982 and 1984 and has since included performances across the Greater Toronto Area and on Parliament Hill.

Having previously celebrated Kishore Kumar’s 93rd birth anniversary at the same venue, the organizers announced plans for a larger celebration at the Vedic Cultural Centre for the Kishore Kumar Centenary.
